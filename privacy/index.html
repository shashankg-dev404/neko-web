Privacy Policy

Neko — Anime & Manga Tracker   |   Effective date: May 25, 2026

Neko ("we", "our", or "the app") is a free mobile application that helps you track anime and manga using the AniList platform. This policy explains what data we collect, why we collect it, and how we handle it.

Neko operates a small backend on Cloudflare Workers to deliver push notifications, receive in-app feedback, and record anonymous aggregate usage events. Everything else — your anime/manga lists, search index, image cache, and reading progress — stays on your device. This policy distinguishes the two clearly.

2. Data We Do NOT Collect

  • We do not collect your real name, password, or AniList login credentials. OAuth handles authentication entirely on AniList's website.
  • We do not use third-party analytics SDKs (Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, Mixpanel, Amplitude, etc.), tracking pixels, or advertising SDKs. The aggregate usage events described in §8 are written to our own Cloudflare backend, not to any third-party analytics service.
  • We do not collect your advertising identifier, location, contact list, phone number, microphone, or camera data.
  • We do not sell, share, or transfer any personal data to third parties for marketing or any other purpose.
  • We do not read or store the contents of any search you run — including recommendation search, mood shortcuts, and (in versions that had it) the on-device Smart Search. The corresponding analytics events record only a bucketed result count (e.g., "1-5", "6-20") and a success flag; the query text itself is never sent. Smart Search was removed from the app in August 2026 and ran entirely on your device for as long as it existed.
  • We do not log full IP addresses on our backend.
  • We do not collect any screenshots — there is no screenshot upload feature in this version of Neko.

3. How Authentication Works

Neko uses AniList OAuth (Implicit Grant) to authenticate you. When you tap "Login with AniList", you are redirected to AniList's website where you enter your credentials directly. Neko never sees or stores your AniList password. We only receive a temporary access token that allows us to read and update your anime/manga lists.

4. Data Storage Locations

Local data (your lists, cached metadata, OAuth token, Smart Search index, image cache) is stored only on your device using the platform's secure storage and SQLite database. It never leaves your device unless you take an action that explicitly sends it (e.g., subscribing to notifications, submitting feedback, opening the app for the periodic app_open analytics event).

Server-side data (listed in §1.2 above) is stored on Cloudflare D1, Cloudflare's edge-distributed SQLite database, or, for the aggregate usage events in §8, on Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine. The infrastructure is operated by Cloudflare, Inc. and inherits Cloudflare's security and certifications. We do not run our own physical servers.

5. Push Notifications

If you enable push notifications, Neko polls the AniList API on a fixed schedule (hourly for new episode airing, every 6 hours for status changes such as a series returning from hiatus) and sends a push notification through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging service to the devices that have subscribed to that title.

You can disable notifications at any time from your device's system settings or from Neko's Settings → Notifications screen. You can also turn off individual subscriptions one at a time from the bell icon on each title's detail page.

6. In-App Feedback

Neko has a "Send feedback" option in Settings that lets you message us directly. When you submit feedback:

  • Your message body and the diagnostics listed in §1.2 are stored in our Cloudflare D1 database.
  • If you provided an email address, your submission is also forwarded to the maintainer's inbox so a human can reply.
  • The email you provide is used only to reply to your specific feedback. We will never add it to a mailing list, sell it, share it, or use it for analytics.
  • Feedback submissions, including any optional email, are retained for 90 days after the report is resolved, or 180 days for unresolved reports, after which they are deleted automatically. Aggregate, non-identifying summaries (e.g., "12 users reported X") may be kept indefinitely.

The feedback flow has no "send a copy to yourself" feature, and we do not send automated email replies — any reply you receive will be from a real person.

7. Trailer Playback (YouTube)

Neko's "Coming Soon" feed shows an inline Play button on anime cards that have a publicly available trailer on YouTube. No video data is loaded until you tap Play — at that point, an embedded YouTube player is opened inside the app to stream the trailer.

The embedded player is provided by Google LLC via the publicly documented YouTube IFrame Player API. When you tap Play, the following happens:

  • The YouTube player loads inside an in-app web view from youtube.com and googlevideo.com.
  • YouTube/Google receives standard request metadata associated with playing an embedded video, including your IP address, device user-agent, the video ID being played, and any YouTube cookies already present on your device (e.g., if you are signed into YouTube in your phone's Chrome browser).
  • YouTube may use this data for the purposes described in Google's own Privacy Policy, including anonymous view-count aggregation and, if you are signed in to a Google account, attribution to your YouTube watch history.

Neko itself does not receive, store, or share any video playback data with Google or any other party — the data flow is directly between the embedded YouTube player and Google's servers. Neko has no analytics hook on the trailer play action.

If you do not want Google to receive this data, do not tap the Play button on a Coming Soon card. The rest of the app (browsing, tracking, reminders, smart search) works fully without ever invoking the YouTube embed.

8. Anonymous Usage Analytics

Neko sends a small set of anonymous usage events to its own Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine to understand how the app is used in aggregate. This data is used only to inform product decisions (e.g., "does anyone use the forum?", "how many users complete a MyAnimeList import?", "which screens do signed-out users get stuck on?") and is never sold, shared, or used to track you individually.

The events sent are limited to the following fixed allow-list — anything outside this list is silently dropped by the server. Every value in brackets is a fixed category chosen from a short predefined set, never free text: no search queries, no anime or manga titles, no thread or comment contents, no usernames, and no identifiers that could point back to a specific piece of content you looked at.

Opening and using the app

  • app_open — fired when you open the app. Deduplicated to at most once per hour per device, so foregrounding the app repeatedly does not send extra events. Records whether you are signed in.
  • app_resume — fired when you return to the app after at least 30 minutes away. Records how long you were away as a coarse band (30m-2h, 2h-24h, 1-7d, 7d+) and whether you are signed in.
  • screen_view — fired the first time you reach a given screen in a session. Records the screen's internal route name (a fixed list, e.g. HomeScreen) and whether you are signed in. It records which screen, never what was on it.
  • onboarding_step — which step of the first-run introduction you reached.

Signing in

  • signin_prompt_view — fired when a "sign in" prompt is actually shown to you. Records which surface showed it (e.g. home, forum, mylist).
  • sign_in_start — fired when you begin signing in. Records which prompt or screen you started from.
  • sign_in_complete — fired when sign-in finishes successfully. Records nothing else; your AniList username is not sent.
  • signin_prompt_dismiss — fired when you dismiss a sign-in prompt. Records which surface, and whether that dismissal was the one that stops us asking again.
  • gate_toast — fired when you tap a control that needs an account and we explain why. Records which kind of action you were attempting (e.g. likeThread), so we can tell which features signed-out users actually want.

Your list

  • list_view — fired when you open your list screen. Deduplicated per app-launch session.
  • details_view — fired when you open an anime or manga details screen. Deduplicated per app-launch session. Which title you opened is not sent.
  • list_add — fired when you add a title to your list. Records the entry point used and whether it was anime or manga.
  • list_progress_inc — fired when you increment your progress from a list card. Records anime/manga, the control used, and whether that increment completed the series.
  • list_custom_use — custom-list usage. Records the action (available, view, assign), anime/manga, and a count. Your custom list names are never sent.
  • list_import_complete — fired when a MyAnimeList → AniList import finishes. Includes a bucketed list-size category (one of: 0, 1-19, 20-99, 100-499, 500+) and a success/empty outcome.
  • list_export_complete — fired when an export file is actually written to your device. Records the format (MyAnimeList XML or CSV) and a bucketed size. The file's contents never leave your device.

Discovery

  • rec_search_run — fired when you run a recommendation search. Includes a bucketed result count (one of: 0, 1-5, 6-20, 21+), a success flag, anime/manga, and which entry point you used. Your actual search query text is never sent.
  • mood_search_used — fired when you use a mood shortcut. Records which mood and a bucketed result count.
  • for_you_view, for_you_tap, for_you_hide, for_you_not_interested — how the personalised rows on Home are used: whether they were shown, which row was tapped, and which rows you turned off. The row is identified by its type (e.g. airing, sequels), never by the titles in it.
  • schedule_open — fired when you open the airing schedule. Records which control you used to get there.
  • shared_taste_open — fired when you open the "titles you both have" comparison from another user's profile. Records nothing else — not the other user, not the titles.

Forum

  • forum_thread_open — fired when you open a forum thread. Records only which part of the app you came from (e.g. board, following, details). The thread's identity, title and contents are never sent.
  • forum_engage — fired when you successfully like, like a reply to, follow, reply to, or create a thread. Records only which of those five actions it was. No thread, comment, text or identity is sent.

Notifications and reliability

  • subscription_created — fired when you subscribe to alerts for a title. Which title is not sent here (the subscription itself is covered in §1.2).
  • notification_tap — fired when you open a notification.
  • api_rate_limited — fired when AniList or Jikan rate-limits one of our requests, so we can tell how often you are being slowed down. Records the service, the status code and which request type. Purely technical; contains nothing about you.

Retired events. Older versions of Neko sent smart_search_run, smart_search_enabled, smart_search_ready and smart_search_query for the on-device Smart Search feature. Smart Search was removed from the app in August 2026 and newer versions send none of these. They remain on the server's allow-list only so that events from older installs, which are still supported, continue to be accepted rather than rejected. As before, no query text was ever sent by any of them — only a bucketed result count and a success flag.

Each event is tagged with the same anonymous device identifier used for push notifications and feedback (see §1.2). The events are stored in Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine for approximately 30 days, after which they are auto-purged by Cloudflare's retention policy. We do not use Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, Mixpanel, Amplitude, or any third-party analytics SDK — the analytics endpoint is on the same Cloudflare backend already described elsewhere in this policy.

There is no in-app toggle to disable analytics in this version of Neko because the payload contains no personally identifiable information, no search query text, no title names, and is auto-purged within ~30 days. To stop sending events entirely, uninstall the app — uninstalling removes the anonymous device identifier and ends the data flow.

11. Data Deletion & Your Rights

You can delete all of your data at any time. Different categories have different deletion paths:

  • Local data on your device — log out within the app (clears your access token) or uninstall Neko (removes all locally stored data).
  • Server-side notification & subscription data — open Neko → Settings → Notifications → Delete my data. This calls our backend and erases your device record, all subscriptions, and your notification history within seconds.
  • Server-side feedback submissions — email neko.app.contact@gmail.com with the subject line "Delete my feedback". We will confirm within 30 days. If you remember your anonymous device identifier (visible in Settings → About) or the email you used on the feedback form, include it so we can find your records quickly.
  • Aggregate usage events (§8) — these are auto-deleted by Cloudflare after ~30 days and contain no personally identifiable information; for that reason there is no on-demand deletion path. If you want them gone sooner, uninstall the app — the data flow stops immediately, and the existing ~30-day rolling window flushes itself.
  • Your AniList authorization — revoke Neko's access from your AniList App Settings. This invalidates the OAuth token immediately.

EU / UK users have additional rights under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Articles 15–22: access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection, and restriction. To exercise any of these rights, email us at the address above. Our legal basis for processing the data in §1.2 is legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — specifically, operating push notifications, responding to user-reported issues, and improving the app via aggregate usage analytics. You may object at any time using the deletion paths above.

12. Data Retention Summary

  • Device records, FCM tokens — kept while the install is active; auto-pruned 30 days after we detect the token has become invalid (typically after uninstall).
  • Subscriptions — kept until you unsubscribe or your device record is deleted.
  • Notification history — kept for approximately 90 days.
  • Feedback submissions — 90 days after resolution, or 180 days if unresolved, then auto-deleted.
  • Anonymous usage events (§8) — approximately 30 days, then auto-purged by Cloudflare Analytics Engine.
  • Backups — purged within 30 days of any deletion.
